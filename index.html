Case Study 01 · Luxury Hospitality · Digital Brand System

Sandals & Beaches
Resort Ecosystem

Client
Unique Vacations / RSAB IT India
Duration
2016 – Present
Role
Web Graphic UI/UX Designer
Tools
Adobe XD · Photoshop · Figma
The Problem

A growing brand family with fragmented digital presence

Sandals Resorts and its sub-brands operated with varying degrees of visual consistency across web, email, and print. As the family expanded across the Caribbean, maintaining a cohesive luxury-first digital identity became increasingly complex.

6+
Sub-brands
9yr
Engagement
All
Web · Mobile · Print
Research & Discovery

Understanding the luxury traveller

Audited all digital assets across properties — mapping inconsistencies in colour, typography, imagery, and component patterns. Competitor benchmarking against Four Seasons, Aman, and Belmond informed visual targets. Research revealed affluent guests responded to immersive imagery, clear value communication, and friction-free navigation.

Design Process

Building a scalable design system

1
Brand audit & asset inventory
Catalogued all existing digital assets, identified visual inconsistencies, and defined a hierarchy of brand elements across the family.
2
Design system definition
Established shared typographic scales, colour tokens, spacing systems, and component patterns — adapted per sub-brand.
3
Web UI redesign
Designed new look and feel for sandals.com and beaches.com — from hero layouts and navigation to room galleries and booking entry points.
4
Marketing & campaign design
Delivered ongoing e-mailer campaigns, promotional brochures, social assets, and print materials at high velocity.
5
Dev collaboration & QA
Partnered with development through implementation — reviewing builds, providing redlines, iterating on technical constraints.
Outcome

"A unified visual language across 6 Caribbean resort brands — delivering luxury brand consistency at every digital touchpoint, from the first Google impression to the booking confirmation email."

Case Study 02 · Hospitality Tech · Mobile App UX

Houdini Hotel
Automation App

Client
Arowana Consulting Ltd
Duration
2013 – 2016
Role
Senior UI/UX Designer
Tools
Sketch · Just in Mind · Photoshop
The Problem

Hotel room control — without the learning curve

Hotel guests interact with room controls through fragmented physical interfaces varying room to room. Houdini sought to consolidate all controls — lighting, AC, curtains, TV, room service — into a single, beautiful mobile experience for iOS and Android, serving both tech-savvy and first-time smartphone users alike.

iOS
iPhone & iPad
And
Android Platform
0
Training Needed
Design Process

From whiteboard to interactive prototype

1
Stakeholder workshops
Facilitated structured sessions with hotel operations to understand backend constraints and must-have control functions.
2
User flow mapping
Created detailed journey maps for 6 primary guest scenarios — check-in, sleep, wake, room service, checkout, emergency.
3
Wireframing in Sketch
Low and mid-fidelity wireframes for all screens across iOS and Android, accounting for platform-specific interaction patterns.
4
Interactive prototype
Built fully clickable prototype in Just in Mind for usability testing with real hotel guests before visual design was finalised.
5
Visual design & handoff
Applied the final premium, minimal visual language with clear iconography and gesture-led controls, then delivered detailed specs.
Outcome

"A zero-learning-curve hotel room app that guests picked up and used intuitively within seconds — reducing front-desk calls and improving in-stay satisfaction scores."

Airline Booking
Engine UI

Duration

The Problem

Booking flows that lost passengers before they converted

Both airlines needed booking engine pages redesigned to reduce drop-off, improve visual clarity, and align with brand identities within existing technical infrastructure. A confusing step or unclear error message could mean an abandoned session worth hundreds of dollars.

2
Airlines redesigned
All
Booking steps
↑

Design Process

Redesigning the critical path

1
UX audit & friction mapping

2
Flow restructuring
Rationalised the step sequence — reducing screens required while surfacing key decisions at optimal moments.
3
Component redesign
Redesigned date pickers, flight result cards, fare tables, passenger forms, and seat maps for visual clarity and touch-friendliness.
4
Brand alignment
Faithfully reflected each airline's visual identity within a consistent, conversion-optimised UI framework.

Responsive adaptation
Designed mobile-optimised versions of all critical booking screens for seamless on-the-go completion.

Case Study 05 · Public Sector · Kiosk Touch UI

Southern Railway
Passenger Kiosk

Client
Southern Railway (Indian Railways)
Period
2012 – 2013
Role
Senior UI Designer
Tools
Photoshop · Illustrator · Flash
The Problem

Serving millions of passengers — with zero training

Indian Railways serves over 23 million passengers daily. The UI had to work for an extraordinarily diverse user base — different literacy levels, languages, ages, and technology familiarity — immediately comprehensible to any passenger with no instruction required.

2
User groups
∞
User diversity
0
Instructions
Design Process

Built for a challenging physical environment

1
Environment analysis
Visited station environments to understand lighting conditions, kiosk placement, user approach angles, and peak usage patterns.
2
User flow definition
Mapped the minimum taps required for each key task — targeting a maximum of 3 interactions to result for PNR check, train status, platform lookup.
3
Large-format visual design
Designed in Photoshop and Illustrator for large touch screen dimensions — bold typography, high contrast, oversized touch targets.
4
Animation & motion design
Used Macromedia Flash to design transitions and idle-state animations — engaging during low-traffic periods with clear interaction feedback.
5
Accessibility review
Reviewed for legibility at distance, colour contrast compliance, and logical information hierarchy without relying on colour alone.
Outcome

"A touch screen information system designed to serve one of the world's most diverse passenger populations — intuitively, inclusively, and without a single line of instruction."
